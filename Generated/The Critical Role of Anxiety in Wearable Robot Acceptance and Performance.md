# The Critical Role of Anxiety in Wearable Robot Acceptance and Performance: A Biological and Psychological Synthesis

## Executive Summary

Anxiety emerges as a pivotal psychobiological factor determining the success or failure of human-wearable robot integration. Operating through predictive processing frameworks, anxiety fundamentally disrupts the sensorimotor integration necessary for embodiment, trust formation, and functional performance in assistive robotics.

## Neurobiological Mechanisms

Anxiety manifests biologically through altered somatosensory cortex activity, particularly in the right postcentral gyrus, which directly processes bodily sensations ([[Why Anxiety?#Somatosensory Cortices]], #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety/bio/somatosensory_cortex). The somatosensory cortex participates in emotional regulation and exhibits structural and functional changes in anxiety disorders ([[Target word 'somatosensory_cortex and anxiety or proprioception']], Higher-Order Roles Perception Emotion Social, #cognitive/anxiety/trait_anxiety, #somatosensory_cortex/emotional_regulation). This neural architecture creates a direct pathway through which anxiety disrupts the proprioceptive feedback essential for wearable robot control—proprioception being the sensory modality most affected by wearable robots and carrying the most weight in system performance ([[Why Anxiety?#^9b4e49]], #proprioception). ^tr-4z9f7llbo

The mechanism operates through norepinephrine modulation of prediction error minimization in active inference frameworks ([[Target word 'anxiety or threat or safety or risk and (somatosensory_cortex or gyrus or frontal lobe or M1 or M2 or norepinephrine or adrenaline or amygdala or bio or insula) and (embodiment or ownership or agency or proprioception or trust)']], [[A predictive processing perspective of disrupted motor control under anxiety]]). Anxiety recasts the brain into a state of hypervigilance where the world is viewed as more uncertain and the agent can no longer predict the absence of threats with confidence ([[TagsRoutes/reports/TagReport_#cognitive__anxiety]], #cognitive/anxiety/hypervigilance section). This high-level belief in unpredictability influences precision weighting across the cortical hierarchy, initially causing prediction-making to become less certain and sensory errors to dominate ([[TagsRoutes/reports/TagReport_#cognitive__anxiety|TagReport_#cognitive__anxiety]], #cognitive/anxiety/unpredictability section). ^tr-6ynjvy4ka

## Direct Impact on Wearable Robot Performance

The empirical evidence is unambiguous: state anxiety after exoskeleton use predicted higher perceived workload (NASA-TLX total score, Adjusted R² = 0.45, p = 0.02), while trait anxiety predicted perceived effort (Adjusted R² = 0.43, p = 0.02) ([[Human–Robot Interactions A Pilot Study of Psychoaffective and Cognitive Factors to Boost the Acceptance and Usability of Assistive Wearable Devices#Key Findings]]). This demonstrates that both transient and enduring anxiety states directly quantify user experience and system acceptance.

Critically, anxiety disrupts the motor automaticity essential for skilled exoskeleton use through sensorimotor mismatch—defined as incongruent feedback between sensory and motor systems that amplifies errors and impairs movement performance ([[TagsRoutes/reports/TagReport_#trust_factors|TagReport_#trust_factors]], #motor_control/motor_performance section, #motor_control/motor_performance, #trust_factors/robot_factors/performance, #prediction_error). According to execution focus models, anxiety increases mental effort and conscious movement monitoring, which disrupts automatic control processes, while the constrained action hypothesis suggests anxiety-induced internal focus impairs unconscious, reflexive control processes regulating coordinated movement ([[TagsRoutes/reports/TagReport_#cognitive__anxiety|TagReport_#cognitive__anxiety]], #cognitive/anxiety section, #cognitive/anxiety, #motor_control, #models/execution_focus_models, #cognitive/anxiety/mental_effort). ^tr-ilvfx1age

## Proprioceptive and Sensory Integration Breakdown

Anxiety biases predictive processing, shifting attention and weighting of sensory signals, yielding either rigid or overly variable movements and critically reducing trust in sensory feedback (Target word 'anxiety or threat or safety or risk', [[Anxiety and Proprioception]], Theoretical work, #cognitive/anxiety/predictive_processing_bias, #Sensory_Processing/sensory_integration, #cognitive/anxiety/rigid_movements, #cognitive/anxiety/overly_variable_movements, #cognitive/anxiety/reduced_trust_in_sensory_feedback). In healthy young adults, higher anxiety correlated with greater postural sway and altered reliance on somatosensory and visual input, indicating less efficient sensory integration (Target word 'cognitive or perception', t3, #Sensory_Processing/sensory_integration, #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety, #cognitive/anxiety/greater_sway). High-anxiety patients demonstrated delayed sensory reweighting when proprioception was perturbed, leading to greater postural instability (Target word 'anxiety or threat or safety or risk', [[Anxiety and Proprioception]], t2, #cognitive/anxiety/postural_instability, #Sensory_Processing/precision). ^tr-ojzv6m77q

This is catastrophic for wearable robots because proprioception acts as the sensory mediator for the "Sense of Agency"—without precise proprioceptive feedback, the user cannot achieve the psychological state of "human-in-the-loop" trust ([[Why Anxiety?#Embodiment and Proprioception]]). Proprioception is richly represented in the somatosensory cortex, especially S1, where it is integrated with touch and used to update internal body representations (Why Anxiety?, [[Somatosensory Cortices]], #somatosensory_cortex). When anxiety disrupts this integration, embodiment—the sense of ownership and agency critical for natural movement, intuitive control, and effective rehabilitation—becomes impossible (Target word 'cognitive or perception', 1 Embodiment Drives Functional Success, #perception/safety/factors/human_factors/sense_of_agency, #perception/safety/factors/human_factors/sense_of_ownership). ^tr-r3ipyd9pr

## The Trust-Anxiety Bidirectional Loop

From evolutionary and psychological perspectives, anxiety functions as a threat-detection system that evolved to keep people vigilant to potential threats, especially social rejection, status loss, or aggression (Target word 'cognitive or perception', What Anxiety Is For Evolutionary Biological, #threat). Early environments shape a generally "threat-sensitive" anxiety system; high baseline anxiety then makes people more wary and less trusting of others (Target word 'anxiety or threat or safety or risk', Direction of Causality, #evolution, #cognitive/anxiety, #threat, #trust_factors/human_factors/traits/anxiety). This creates a vicious cycle: anxiety reduces trust, and reduced trust amplifies anxiety. Meta-analytic evidence confirms that higher attachment anxiety associates with lower interpersonal trust (Why Anxiety?, How Trust and Anxiety Interact Psychologically). Longitudinal data in older adults suggest higher bonding trust predicts lower trait anxiety over time, partly via better self-rated health (Why Anxiety?, Direction of Causality, #trust_factors/human_factors/traits/anxiety). ^tr-6xp9tg9l6

Neurobiologically, this manifests through the amygdala and anterior insula. The amygdala codes emotional salience and its activity correlates with subjective trustworthiness evaluations; amygdala structure may be a neural construct associated with individual differences in the tendency to trust and distrust (Why Anxiety?, Amygdala, #important, #trust_factors/human_factors/traits/amygdala_structure). The anterior insula codes both trust and distrust evaluations of faces (Target word 'anxiety or threat or safety or risk', Trust in Human-Robot Interaction, #trust_bio/insula). Favorable trust assessments lead to less amygdala activation (Why Anxiety?, Amygdala, #measures/objective_measures/brain_scan, #trust_bio/amygdala).

Trust acts as a modulator for embodiment; higher trust facilitates neural integration of robotic feedback, effectively "sharpening" the user's perceived proprioceptive control (Target word 'anxiety or threat or safety or risk', trust-embodiment, #proprioception). Conversely, anxiety degrades this trust-embodiment coupling, creating a feedback loop where poor embodiment increases anxiety, which further degrades embodiment.

## Cognitive Trust Formation and Performance-Based Metrics

Cognitive trust—based on the robot's reliability and functionality—evolves into affective trust as users experience more natural, embodied interaction supported by continuous sensory feedback and adaptive control (Why Anxiety?, Cognitive Trust). However, anxiety disrupts this evolutionary pathway at the foundational level. The cognitive perception of safety is highly affected by anxiety (Why Anxiety?, Cognitive aspect), and this safety perception must precede affective trust development.

The definition of robot "performance" is shifting from purely mechanical metrics to human-machine fluency measured by skill-normalized performance and co-adaptation success (Why Anxiety?, The definition of "performance"). Expert performance is reached when the user's visuomotor system has fully "internalized" the robot's physics, measured by movement trajectory stability and decreased visual attention (Why Anxiety?, The definition of "performance"). Visuomotor skill level predicts learning capacity; therefore, robot performance is defined by the speed of the user's "proprioceptive recalibration" and reduction in visual error-correction effort (Why Anxiety?, Claim, #trust_factors/robot_factors/performance). Anxiety sabotages this entire process—during early motor learning, anxious learners cope with high uncertainty by constraining movements into rigid, stereotyped patterns that prevent skill development ([[TagsRoutes/reports/TagReport_#trust_factors__human_factors|TagReport_#trust_factors__human_factors]], #cognitive/anxiety section, #cognitive/anxiety, #motor_control/motor_learning, #trust_factors/robot_factors/uncertainty, #cognitive/anxiety/unpredictability). ^tr-13ru2144k

## Modifiable Intervention Point: Familiarization

The data reveal a critical intervention pathway: pre-trial anxiety was significantly higher than post-trial anxiety, showing that familiarization reduces anxiety (Target word 'cognitive or perception', Key Findings, #trust_factors/human_factors/familiarity, #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety/factors/familiarization). This empirically validates that anxiety—while biologically rooted—is situationally modifiable. Objective measures, particularly physiological data, can predict perceived safety, highlighting the necessity of transparency and familiarity in robot design to foster safer and more acceptable human-robot collaborations (Target word 'cognitive or perception', [[Summary]], #measures/objective_measures/perception/safety_measure, #trust_factors/human_factors/familiarity, #trust_factors/human_factors/transparency). ^tr-u4h7sdihl

## Conclusion

Anxiety is not merely a confounding variable in wearable robotics—it is a central organizing principle that determines whether human-robot integration succeeds or fails. Operating through norepinephrine-mediated precision modulation in predictive processing frameworks, anxiety disrupts the somatosensory-motor integration necessary for embodiment. It degrades proprioceptive accuracy, delays sensory reweighting, increases postural instability, and constrains movements into maladaptive patterns. Through the amygdala-insula network, anxiety bidirectionally couples with trust, creating feedback loops that either amplify or attenuate system acceptance. The quantifiable impacts—45% variance in workload, 43% variance in perceived effort—are too substantial to ignore. Wearable robot design must therefore prioritize anxiety reduction through familiarization protocols, transparent operation, and predictable behavior. The biological reality is stark: you cannot achieve embodiment, trust, or functional performance in the presence of sustained anxiety. The challenge for wearable robotics is not purely engineering—it is neuroaffective engineering.
