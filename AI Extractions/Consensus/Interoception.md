Interoception is the process by which the nervous system senses, interprets, integrates, and regulates signals originating from within the body, providing a continuous mapping of the body's internal physiological state. 

It involves afferent signaling from the body to the brain through neural and humoral pathways, neural encoding and integration of this information, and its influence on cognition, behavior, and conscious feelings. 
Interoception plays a crucial role in maintaining physiological homeostasis, motivating behaviors, shaping emotional experiences, and contributing to the psychological sense of self. 

It is implicated in various health conditions, including mental disorders like anxiety, depression, eating disorders, and somatic symptom disorders. 

The complexity of interoception arises from its multimodal nature and integration with external sensory inputs, making it challenging to measure and manipulate directly in humans. 

Research continues to refine its definitions and explore its neurobiological mechanisms and clinical implications (Chen et al., 2021; Quadt et al., 2018; Participants, 2017; Quigley et al., 2021; Desmedt et al., 2023; Ceunen et al., 2016).
