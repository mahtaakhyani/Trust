The sense of **body ownership** arises from the integration of multiple sensory signals, including visual, tactile, proprioceptive, and interoceptive inputs, combined with prior beliefs about the body.

Multisensory congruence in space and time—such as synchronous visuotactile or visuomotor signals—is crucial for generating a strong feeling that a body part or whole body belongs to oneself (Ehrsson, 2020; Chancel et al., 2021; O'Kane et al., 2024). ^1

Individual differences in how sensory uncertainty and prior expectations are weighted influence the strength of ownership experiences (Chancel et al., 2021). The **visual appearance** and personalization of a body or avatar also significantly enhance ownership, with more realistic or self-like representations increasing embodiment and presence (Waltemate et al., 2018; Pyasik et al., 2020). 

Additionally, affective interoceptive signals like pleasant touch contribute to ownership but may interact with exteroceptive cues depending on context and individual sensitivity (Crucianelli et al., 2017). 

Across the lifespan, body ownership remains stable despite physical changes, possibly due to an increased reliance on top-down processes and compensatory sensory recalibration between vision and proprioception (Weijs et al., 2024; Martinelli et al., 2025).

In Summary, body ownership is shaped by dynamic multisensory integration governed by spatial-temporal congruence, sensory precision, prior beliefs, visual realism, interoceptive signals, and developmental factors.
