### While **anxiety can alter balance and body perception**, evidence that it directly impairs basic proprioceptive accuracy is **mixed and task‑dependent**.

## 1. Direct tests of proprioceptive accuracy

^a442f9

Lab studies that explicitly measure joint position sense generally _do not_ find a strong effect of acute anxiety or stress on proprioceptive accuracy:

- In a randomized trial, an online stressor increased state anxiety but **did not change elbow joint position reproduction accuracy** ([[#^2be325|Koncz et al., 2025]]). #proprioception/elbow
- A placebo/nocebo experiment manipulating expectations and measuring state anxiety also found **no effect on actual joint position accuracy**, though participants _felt_ their proprioception had changed ([[#^86ebe8|Horváth et al., 2024]]). #perception/proprioception ^tr-gtel329xy

At the ankle, exam-related stress with elevated cortisol and higher state anxiety was linked to **worse ankle joint position sense**, but only with eyes closed and with **small correlations** ([[#^8dba2d|Şenol et al., 2019]]). #cognitive/stress #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety #cognitive/anxiety/bio/cortisol #proprioception/ankle ^tr-127f6ed38

## 2. Anxiety, balance, and sensory integration

Even when basic joint sense is preserved, anxiety can affect how proprioceptive input is **used for balance**:

- In healthy young adults, higher anxiety correlated with **greater sway** and altered reliance on somatosensory and visual input, suggesting less efficient sensory integration ([[#^c3d1e1|Marques et al., 2025]]). #Sensory_Processing/sensory_integration #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety #cognitive/anxiety/greater_sway ^t3
- In chronic stroke, high-anxiety patients showed **delayed sensory reweighting** when proprioception was perturbed (Achilles tendon vibration), leading to greater postural instability ([[#^a37d58|Jamali et al., 2019]]). #cognitive/anxiety/postural_instability #Sensory_Processing/precision  ^t2
- Theoretical work proposes that anxiety biases predictive processing, shifting attention and weighting of sensory signals, which can yield either **rigid or overly variable movements** and reduced trust in sensory feedback ([[#^be6a7d|Harris et al., 2023]]; [[#^bfab18|Nieuwenhuys & Oudejans, 2017]]). #cognitive/anxiety/predictive_processing_bias #Sensory_Processing/sensory_integration #Sensory_Processing/sensory_integration #cognitive/anxiety/rigid_movements #cognitive/anxiety/overly_variable_movements #cognitive/anxiety/reduced_trust_in_sensory_feedback ^t1 ^tr-5uaqwduya

## Conclusion

Anxiety does not consistently degrade raw proprioceptive acuity in simple joint-position tasks, but higher anxiety and stress can slightly worsen proprioception in demanding conditions (e.g., no vision) and more clearly disrupt **balance and the integration of proprioceptive signals** into motor control.
