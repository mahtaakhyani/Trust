## **Somatosensory Cortices: What They Do** ^tr-i071en4ez

The somatosensory cortices (primary S1 and secondary S2/SII) are key hubs for turning touch and body signals into perception, pain, movement control, and even emotion. #somatosensory_cortex   ^1
> [!note] - The **postcentral gyrus** is the anatomical location of the [[Neuroanatomy, Somatosensory Cortex|primary somatosensory cortex (S1)]] #somatosensory_cortex/postcentral_gyrus #somatosensory_cortex ^tr-3gerv19gt

## **Core Sensory Functions**

- S1 and S2 process **tactile, pressure, thermal, and nociceptive (pain)** inputs from skin, muscles, and joints ([[#^a3580e|Xu et al., 2025]]; [[#^102f57|Kropf et al., 2018]]; [[#^9814ca|Lamorie-Foote et al., 2024]]). #somatosensory_cortex^so
- S1 is especially important for **fine discrimination** of mechanical and cooling stimuli and setting sensory thresholds ([[#^a3580e|Xu et al., 2025]]; [[#^9c8180|Ziegler et al., 2023]]; [[#^0826cf|Taub et al., 2024]]; [[#^e92f97|Liang et al., 2011]]). #somatosensory_cortex/s1 #Sensory_Processing 
- S1 is the "first stop" for sensory signals (touch, pain, temperature) coming from the thalamus [Ch5, Somatosensory Processes](https://nba.uth.tmc.edu/neuroscience/m/s2/chapter05.html) #Sensory_Processing  #somatosensory_cortex/s1 #somatosensory_cortex 
- S2 contributes strongly to **mechanical and heat sensitivity** and helps set basal sensory thresholds via its outputs to secondary motor cortex (M2) ([[#^0826cf|Taub et al., 2024]]; [[#^8b1e1d|Taub et al., 2022]]). #motor_control/motor_cortex/M2 #somatosensory_cortex/s2 #Sensory_Processing

## **Pain and Defensive Signaling**

- Both S1 and S2 are central to **pain perception**, including location and intensity of pain ([[#^9c8180|Ziegler et al., 2023]]; [[#^69bf68|Bushnell et al., 1999]]; [[#^368938|Cauda et al., 2012]]; [[#^a35e70|Song et al., 2021]]; [[#^e92f97|Liang et al., 2011]]).
- Layer-specific S1 circuits can either **enhance or suppress pain behaviors** (L6 pronociceptive, L5 antinociceptive) ([[#^9c8180|Ziegler et al., 2023]]).
- S2–S1 circuits mediate **“pain inhibits pain”** (one pain reducing another), providing cortical mechanisms of analgesia ([[#^12b52a|Huang et al., 2025]]). 
> [!note]- Analgesia 
> Analgesia is the reduction or elimination of pain, enabling relief without loss of consciousness

## **Higher-Order Roles: Perception, Emotion, Social**

- S2 encodes a continuum from **pure sensory** to **perceptual/choice-related** activity, linking touch to decisions and categories ([[#^f677b0|Condylis et al., 2020]]; [[#^2ad429|Rossi-Pool et al., 2021]] ).
- Somatosensory cortex supports **body perception, [[Body Ownership]], haptic object recognition, and action guidance** ([[#^9837aa|De Haan & Dijkerman, 2020]]). #somatosensory_cortex/body_perception #perception/proprioception #somatosensory_cortex/sense_of_ownership #somatosensory_cortex/haptic_object_recognition #somatosensory_cortex/action_guidance
- It participates in **emotional regulation** and shows structural/functional changes in mood and anxiety disorders ([[#^102f57|Kropf et al., 2018]]). #cognitive/anxiety/trait_anxiety #somatosensory_cortex/emotional_regulation
- During social perception, somatosensory cortices are vicariously activated when seeing others’ touch or pain, adding a **somatic dimension to empathy** ([[#^928df3|Keysers et al., 2010]]). #somatosensory_cortex/somatic_dimention_of_empathy ^tr-p7zpusl9v

## **Conclusion**

Somatosensory cortices do far more than “feel touch”: they set sensory thresholds, shape and gate pain, integrate memory and context, support body representation and action, participate in emotion, and contribute to understanding others’ sensations.
