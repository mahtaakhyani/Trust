# Anxiety, **Trust**, and Evolution: How Are They Related?
#trust 
From psychological, biological, and evolutionary perspectives, **anxiety is best seen as a threat‑detection system**, and problems with trust and anxiety tend to **reinforce each other** rather than one being the single root cause of the other.  #cognitive/anxiety #evolution  ^tr-ndvllopz1

## What Anxiety Is For (Evolutionary & Biological)

- General and social anxiety are viewed as evolved systems that keep people vigilant to **potential threats**, especially social rejection, status loss, or aggression (Karasewich & Kuhlmeier, 2019; Gilboa-Schechtman et al., 2014; Yukai, 2023; Haugan, 2023). #threat 
- Social anxiety, in particular, functions as a defense to avoid damaging relationships or being excluded from the group, which historically threatened survival and reproduction (Karasewich & Kuhlmeier, 2019; Gilboa-Schechtman et al., 2014; Yukai, 2023; Haugan, 2023). #cognitive/anxiety/social_anxiety  ^def ^tr-czhnzxvwf

## How Trust and Anxiety Interact Psychologically

- Meta‑analytic evidence shows higher attachment anxiety and avoidance are both associated with **lower interpersonal trust** (Bao et al., 2022).
- Experimental work finds high‑anxiety individuals’ trust decisions rely more on emotional closeness and less on objective partner trustworthiness, indicating biased threat processing rather than pure “lack of trust” alone (Bao et al., 2024). ^bias

## Direction of Causality

- Longitudinal data in older adults suggest **higher bonding trust predicts lower trait anxiety over time**, partly via better self‑rated health (Jhang, 2020). #trust_factors/human_factors/traits/anxiety 
- Evolutionary models emphasize that early environments shape a generally “threat‑sensitive” anxiety system; such high baseline anxiety then makes people more wary and less trusting of others (Karasewich & Kuhlmeier, 2019; Gilboa-Schechtman et al., 2014; Yukai, 2023; Haugan, 2023). #evolution #cognitive/anxiety #threat #trust_factors/human_factors/traits/anxiety ^early ^tr-t4wjnk1jn

## Conclusion

Overall, anxiety is an evolved threat‑alarm system. Early experiences and environments can tune this system to be more or less sensitive, which then shapes how easily trust (or distrust) develops. In everyday life, **low trust can increase anxiety, and high anxiety can make trust harder**, creating a mutual feedback loop rather than a simple one‑way cause. ^conclude
