

State anxiety is primarily associated with altered activity and connectivity in the **somatosensory cortex**, particularly the right postcentral gyrus, which relates to somatic or bodily sensations of anxiety (The somatosensory cortex contributes to conscious bodily awareness) [[The Altered Somatic Brain Network in State Anxiety|(Li et al., 2019)]].  #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety  #somatosensory_cortex  ^tr-hkzhmcz1d

Functional connectivity changes involving this region and other areas such as the **cerebellum** and **frontal cortices** also correlate with state anxiety levels, highlighting the role of **somatic brain networks** ([[#^137375|Li et al., 2019]]) (→ File available at: [[The Altered Somatic Brain Network in State Anxiety]]. #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety/bio/somatic_brain_networks/frontal_cortesis #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety/bio/somatic_brain_networks/cerebellum  
Additionally, state anxiety is linked to functional connectivity within large-scale brain networks, including increased resting-state connectivity in the **salience network** and ventral nodes of the **default mode network (DMN)**, distinguishing it from trait anxiety which involves structural changes (Saviola et al., 2020). #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety/bio/salience_network #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety/bio/DMN ^tr-wwohibp4d

Cortical networks such as the DMN, **dorsal attention network (DAN)**, **frontoparietal network (FPN)**, and **salience network (SN)** show predictive resting-state functional connectivity patterns related to state anxiety (Duan et al., 2020). #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety/bio/DMN #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety/bio/DAN #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety/bio/salience_network #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety/bio/FPN ^tr-4hqmxd69a

The **bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (BNST)** also plays a role in modulating features of anxiety states through distinct subregions that can either promote or reduce anxiety-like behaviors (Kim et al., 2013). #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety/bio/BNST ^tr-que49eyga

Overall, state anxiety involves dynamic interactions between **somatosensory regions** and **large-scale cortical networks responsible for processing bodily sensations and emotional salience** (Li et al., 2019; Saviola et al., 2020; Duan et al., 2020; Kim et al., 2013).

---

The amygdala's involvement in threat processing is anatomically and temporally specific, with subregions responding differently during early threat conditioning versus safety learning (Wen et al., 2022). 

Regarding the anterior insula, research indicates that loss-related activations exceed gain-related activations approximately twofold, reflecting a stronger neural response to potential losses compared to gains, consistent with its role in processing aversive or negative outcomes (Sambuco et al., 2020; MacDonald et al., 2025). The anterior insula also shows increased activation under threat anticipation and correlates with subjective fear and anxiety sensitivity (Sambuco et al., 2020; MacDonald et al., 2025). #cognitive/anxiety/bio/insula  


Overall, while the amygdala responds rapidly and strongly to threat cues relative to safety signals, its activation patterns can vary across studies; meanwhile, the anterior insula reliably shows greater activation for loss versus gain processing by about a factor of **two** (Wen et al., 2022; Herrmann et al., 2016; Sambuco et al., 2020; MacDonald et al., 2025). #cognitive/anxiety/bio/amygdala #cognitive/anxiety/bio/insula
