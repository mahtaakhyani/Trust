Proprioception (sense of body position and movement) is tightly linked to the **primary somatosensory cortex (S1)**, which is a major cortical target and processor of proprioceptive signals.

## Cortical Representation of Proprioception

- In primates, muscle and joint signals ascend via the dorsal column–medial lemniscus pathway to S1, especially **area 3a** (muscle/joint input) and **areas 3b/1** (cutaneous input) (Delhaye et al., 2018; Yamada et al., 2016).
- Area 2 of S1 encodes **whole‑arm kinematics**, not just hand position, indicating a higher‑level representation of limb state (Chowdhury et al., 2019).
- In mice, proprioceptive afferents define a **proprioceptive cortex** spanning S1 and motor cortex; limb movements are encoded as a **spatial direction vector** relating limb to peripersonal space (Alonso et al., 2023).

### Multimodal Integration in S1

- Many S1 neurons, including in classically “unimodal” areas 3a and 3b, respond to both **proprioceptive and cutaneous** stimuli, supporting early multimodal integration rather than strict segregation (Kim et al., 2015; Yamada et al., 2016).

### Role in Body Representation and Motor Control

- S1 activity reflects **recalibration of proprioceptive hand position** when visual and proprioceptive cues conflict, and S1→M1 pathways appear to mediate how updated body estimates influence motor commands (Mirdamadi et al., 2025; Mirdamadi et al., 2024).
- Disrupting S1 with non‑invasive brain stimulation increases proprioceptive variability and alters proprioceptive changes during **skill learning**, confirming that S1 contributes to conscious limb position sense and its plasticity (Mirdamadi et al., 2025; Mirdamadi & Block, 2020).
- S1 and M1 initially receive proprioceptive input in **parallel** during development, then establish more serial S1→M1 processing for some exafferent signals, highlighting S1’s evolving role in sensorimotor circuits (Gómez et al., 2021).

## Conclusion

Proprioception is richly represented in the somatosensory cortex, especially S1, where it is integrated with touch, transformed into limb- and space-based codes, and used to update internal body representations. ^how
