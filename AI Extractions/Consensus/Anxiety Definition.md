Anxiety is generally defined as a feeling of unease, worry, or fear that can be a natural response to threatening or difficult situations, often disappearing when the situation changes or is resolved (Duvall & Roddy, 2020; Silverman & Schalkwyk, 2019). 

It is described as an emotional state marked by apprehension and physiological arousal, such as tension and nervousness, which can become excessive and persistent in anxiety disorders, impairing daily functioning (Craske & Stein, 2017; Saraso, 2013). 

Anxiety differs from fear in that anxiety focuses on potential future threats, while fear relates to immediate danger (Shebelski & Asmundson, 2020). Clinically, anxiety disorders include various conditions like social anxiety disorder, panic disorder, and generalized anxiety disorder, characterized by disproportionate and chronic anxiety that disrupts life (Craske & Stein, 2017; Grzech et al., 2025; Shebelski & Asmundson, 2020).  ^7f3c4d

Philosophical and phenomenological perspectives highlight anxiety as a complex experience involving feelings of uncertainty, vulnerability, and existential concerns beyond just fear (Batiashvili et al., 2025; Gouveia, 2022). 

Anxiety can also be understood as both a temporary emotional state (state anxiety) and a personality trait (trait anxiety), reflecting individual differences in susceptibility to anxious feelings (Spielberger, 2010).

Based on CBT definitions, Anxiety is a complex cognitive, affective, physiological and behavioral response system (i.e., threat mode) that is activated when anticipated events or circumstances are deemed to be highly aversive because they are perceived to be unpredictable, uncontrollable events that could potentially threaten the vital interests of an individual ([Cognitive Therapy of Anxiety Disorders](https://books.google.co.kr/books?id=QpG9NvKh7L0C&pg=PA1&source=gbs_toc_r&cad=2#v=onepage&q&f=false)).
