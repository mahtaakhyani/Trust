### 3.4 Modulation & Reduction: Serotonergic Influence & Adaptive Processing

Serotonin modulates neural pathways linking interoception to anxiety; acute increases can dampen insular responses associated with anxious arousal (Livermore et al., 2024). Adaptive processing—accurate matching between neuroceptive predictions and interoceptive feedback—can help lower anxiety states (Büttiker et al., 2021; Livermore et al., 2024).

## 4. Discussion

The literature consistently supports that **neuroception** is foundational for initiating anxiety by unconsciously detecting environmental threats or safety cues—especially those related to social trust/distrust—triggering autonomic responses before conscious awareness sets in (Paulus & Stein, 2010; Harrison et al., 2021). This process sets the stage for subsequent conscious experiences of anxiety.

**Interoceptive perception** then shapes how these bodily changes are experienced subjectively; individuals with high trait/state anxiety often show increased negative attention to bodily signals or confusion about their meaning (Zoltowski et al., 2024; Clemente et al., 2024; Kiera et al., 2025), which maintains or amplifies anxious feelings.

Neural evidence points to dynamic interactions between these systems: prediction errors arising from mismatches between expected (neuroceptively modeled) body states and actual sensations drive persistent arousal/anxiety via insula-based circuits (Harrison et al., 2021). Pharmacological modulation (e.g., SSRIs) can alter these relationships at the neural level (Livermore et al., 2024).

While both systems are important for understanding how trust/distrust influences anxiety regulation, **neuroception appears more prominent** at the earliest stages—initiating physiological readiness for defense or social engagement—while conscious perception/interoception becomes critical for ongoing maintenance or reduction of anxious states.

## 5. Conclusion

In summary: **Neuroception** is most prominent for initiating/maintaining anxious states through unconscious safety/threat detection (especially regarding trust/distrust), while **conscious perception/interoception** shapes subjective experience/regulation but is less primary as an initiator.
