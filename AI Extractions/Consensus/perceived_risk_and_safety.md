## Perceived Safety
#perception/safety 
Not only is it necessary for robots to operate safely in the same environment as a worker, but it is also critical that workers perceive them as safe. ^tr-cyga1eupf

In the context of HRI, “perceived safety” refers to the user’s perception of danger when interacting with a robot, and the user’s level of comfort during this interaction ([Bartneck et al., 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11076167/#R4)).  ^3b72f7

A lack of perceived safety can affect the **acceptance** of robots in the workplace and can have negative effects on a worker’s mental health. 

In HRI research, perceived safety hasn’t always been directly measured. [Rubagotti et al. (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11076167/#R72) describes concepts related to perceived safety – including **trust**, **comfort**, **fear**, **anxiety**, and **surprise** – and identified four categories of assessment methods in physical HRI experiments: questionnaires, physiological measurements, behavioral assessment, and direct input devices. ^395243

# Measuring Perceived Risk in HRI

- Measuring perceived risk in human-robot interaction (HRI) involves capturing individuals' subjective judgments about potential harm or safety related to robots. 
- While no HRI-specific perceived risk scale is directly described in the available research, related constructs such as **perceived safety** and **risk perception** have been measured using validated questionnaires and multidimensional scales. 
#perception/risk
#perception/safety
- For example, standardized measurement tools for perceived safety in robots have been developed alongside assessments of anthropomorphism, animacy, likeability, and perceived intelligence, using semantic differential scales to ensure **reliability** and **validity**  ([[#^d9c7d4|Bartneck et al., 2009]]). 
- More broadly, perceived risk is often conceptualized as a multidimensional construct including factors like **exposure**, **susceptibility**, **severity**, and **affective response**, with empirically supported subscales developed to measure these dimensions across different hazard contexts  ([[#^381ae7|Walpole & Wilson, 2021]]; [[#^1f3fde|Wilson et al., 2018]]). 
- *These approaches emphasize the importance of **affective reactions** and **perceived severity**, which could be adapted to HRI contexts to assess how users perceive risk from robots.* 
- Additionally, careful item wording and **awareness of biases** are crucial for accurate measurement, as **risk perception can be influenced by heuristics and subjective interpretations**  ([[#^a0c342|Wolff et al., 2019]]).

## Methods ^tr-ewf70alhp

### 1. Validated Subjective Scales

Questionnaires remain the primary method for assessing a user's psychological state post-interaction. 

-  **Perceived Danger (PD) Scale:** A 12-item bifactor scale validated for 2026 that measures four subdimensions: affective states, physical vulnerability, ominousness, and cognitive readiness ([[#^40df53|Molan et al., 2025]]).
-  **Godspeed Series (Perceived Safety):** A widely used semantic differential questionnaire that includes a dedicated dimension for "Perceived Safety" alongside anthropomorphism and likeability ([[#^d9c7d4|Bartneck et al., 2009]]).
- **Robotic Social Attributes Scale (RoSAS):** Assesses dimensions such as "**discomfort**," which serves as a proxy for perceived risk in social contexts ([[#^d7388c|Carpinella et al., 2017]]).
-  **Trust Perception Scale-HRI:** Since trust and risk are inversely related, this scale measures the user's **perceived reliability** and **safety** of the robot ([[#^bed721|Schaefer, 2016]]).

### 2. Physiological Measurements

These provide objective, real-time correlates of internal stress or anxiety that users might not report in surveys. 

-  **Heart Rate Variability (HRV):** Often used to detect changes in stress levels, though results can vary based on individual baseline states ([[#^40df53|Molan et al., 2025]]).
-  **Skin Conductance (GSR):** Measures electrodermal activity as an indicator of physiological arousal during high-risk interactions ([[#^40df53|Molan et al., 2025]]).
-  **Facial Affect Metrics:** Using computer vision to analyze micro-expressions of fear or surprise during the interaction ([[#^d7388c|Carpinella et al., 2017]]).

### 3. Behavioral and Performance Metrics

Observing how a human actually interacts with the robot provides data on their "revealed" risk perception. 

- **Proxemics (Personal Space):** Measuring the physical distance a user maintains from the robot; closer proximity typically indicates lower perceived risk ([[#^40df53|Molan et al., 2025]]).
- **Response Time/Compliance:** Tracking how quickly a user follows a robot's instructions in high-stakes scenarios (e.g., emergency evacuations) ([[#^40df53|Molan et al., 2025]]).
- **Avoidance Behaviors:** Quantifying the frequency of hesitant movements or "retreating" behaviors during robot maneuvers ([[#^40df53|Molan et al., 2025]]). 

### 4. Direct Assessment Tools

- **Pre-task Planning Protocols:** For industrial HRI, tools like the **HRI Safety Risk Assessment Tool** are used to rate hazards before a task begins, combining expert Delphi processes with participant input ([[#^40df53|Molan et al., 2025]]).
- **Direct Input Devices:** Participants can use sliders or pressure sensors in real-time to report "perceived safety" continuously as the robot moves ([[#^40df53|Molan et al., 2025]]).
