##### Sense of Agency
![[[[Proprioception and Sensory Dynamics in Wearable Robotics]]#^why]]
![[[[Proprioception and Sensory Dynamics in Wearable Robotics]]#^w1]]

==Study shows that human–exoskeleton integration can be objectively measured using a neurophysiological marker of embodiment. "Expert" performance is reached when the user’s visuomotor system has fully "internalized" the robot’s physics. Visuomotor skill level is a predictor of learning capacity; therefore, robot performance is defined by the speed of the user’s "proprioceptive recalibration" and the reduction in visual error-correction effort. ==

==Proprioception is the sensory mediator for the "Sense of Agency". Multisensory congruence in space and time—such as synchronous visuotactile or visuomotor signals—is crucial for generating a strong feeling that a body part or whole body belongs to oneself; without precise proprioceptive feedback, the user cannot achieve the psychological state of "human-in-the-loop" trust. Theoretical work proposes that anxiety biases predictive processing, shifting attention and weighting of sensory signals, which can yield either rigid or overly variable movements and reduced trust in sensory feedback.==
==Proprioception is richly represented in the **somatosensory cortex**, especially S1, where it is integrated with touch, transformed into limb- and space-based codes, and used to update internal body representations. State anxiety is also primarily associated with altered activity and connectivity in the somatosensory cortex, particularly the right postcentral gyrus. ==



==Study found that anxiety significantly shapes how people perceive workload when using assistive exoskeletons. State anxiety after use predicted overall workload, while trait anxiety predicted effort. ==
