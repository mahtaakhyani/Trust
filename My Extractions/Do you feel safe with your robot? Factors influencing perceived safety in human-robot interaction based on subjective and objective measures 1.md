# Intro
- A robot that is designed to coexist with humans must be safe not only concerning from causing physical harm but from causing psychological harm.
- perceived safety is crucial for long-term interaction, collaboration, and acceptance. For acceptable HRI, a robot must avoid taking actions that might cause fear, surprise, discomfort or create an unpleasant [social situation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/computer-science/social-situation "Learn more about social situation from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages") for humans even if its actions do not cause any physical harm ([Sisbot et al., 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581921001622?via%3Dihub#bib0055)).
- There may even be a discrepancy between physical safety and safety perception ([Salem et al., 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581921001622?via%3Dihub#bib0051)), and it has been shown that maintenance of physical safety by simply preventing collisions can still lead to a lower degrees of perceived safety ([Lasota and Shah, 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581921001622?via%3Dihub#bib0032)).
- perceived safety during HRI deserves much attention considering the psychological, cognitive, and emotional consequences of interactions. Said differently, *how can we design and implement systems such that the users perceive them safe to interact with?* #important 
- there is nothing to measure in the presence of safety ([Hollnagel, 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581921001622?via%3Dihub#bib0024))

# Definition
The nomenclature for perceived safety varies in different disciplines and [application areas](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/computer-science/application-area "Learn more about application areas from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages"). 
- As an example, the term “**psychological safety**” is used in [work environment](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/social-sciences/work-environment "Learn more about work environment from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages") safety ([Kahn, 1990](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581921001622?via%3Dihub#bib0025)),  team and group dynamics studies ([Edmondson et al., 2004](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581921001622?via%3Dihub#bib0016)). 
- “**Sense of safety and security**” is used in [gerontology](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/gerontology "Learn more about gerontology from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages") literature ([Fonad et al., 2006](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581921001622?via%3Dihub#bib0020))
- and “**perceived safety**” is used in various disciplines ([Raue et al., 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581921001622?via%3Dihub#bib0047)). 
- Similarly, [HRI](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/computer-science/human-robot-interaction "Learn more about HRI from ScienceDirect's AI-generated Topic Pages") literature adopted several different terms for the safety perception: **psychological safety** ([Kamide, Mae, Kawabe, Shigemi, Hirose, Arai, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1145/2157689.2157698), [Lasota, Fong, Shah, et al., 2017]([https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581921001622?via%3Dihub#bib0031](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup?title=A%20survey%20of%20methods%20for%20safe%20human-robot%20interaction&publication_year=2017&author=P.A.%20Lasota&author=T.%20Fong&author=J.A.%20Shah))), **sense of safety and security** ([Akalin et al., 2019a](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-17107-0_12)), **perceived safety** ([Bartneck et al., 2009]([https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581921001622?via%3Dihub#bib0006](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12369-008-0001-3)), **mental safety** ([Matsas and Vosniakos, 2017]([https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581921001622?via%3Dihub#bib0035](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12008-015-0259-2)), and **sense of security** ([Nonaka, Inoue, Arai, Mae, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1109/ROBOT.2004.1307480), [Nyholm, Santamäki-Fischer, Fagerström, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108975537)).

## Key Points
#### Concept and Importance of Perceived Safety in HRI
- **Definition of Perceived safety** = In HRI: **distinct from physical safety** and concerns the **psychological**, **emotional**, and **cognitive** **feeling of safety** during interaction with robots.

- **Crucial** for long-term interaction, user acceptance, and collaboration with robots in everyday and **vulnerable settings** such as **domestic environments,** **healthcare**, and **eldercare**.

## Factors

- Perceived safety is influenced by multiple disciplines and typically associated with factors like: 
	1) **comfort**, 
	2) **experience or familiarity** (enhances perceived safety as users become **confident in the robot’s actions and intentions**), 
	3) **sense of control**, 
	4) **trust** (e.g., no robot system failure and mistakes), 
	5) **predictability** and **transparency** (helping with how users interpret and **foresee** robot actions and **understanding the purpose** behind its behavior).
- Comfort, sense of control, and trust are not only correlated with perceived safety **but also strongly interrelated with each other**, indicating a complex bidirectional relationship in HRI.

#### Findings on Human Characteristics and Perceived Safety
- **Personality** significantly impacts perceived safety; specifically, individuals with low **neuroticism** experience higher perceived safety and sense of control, as neuroticism relates to a tendency for negative emotions and **anxiety**.
- **Extraversion** correlates with higher arousal during interaction but not directly with perceived safety; 
- **Conscientiousness** shows moderate positive correlation with positive emotional valence.
#perception/safety/human_factors/traits/personality ^tr-3bfmzijpe

- **Male** participants generally felt safer, more in control, and reported more positive affective states than **female** participants throughout the interaction, revealing gender differences in safety perception.
#perception/safety/human_factors/traits/gender ^tr-v6ulzc762

- **Unpredictable** robot behaviors of short duration and **not** impairing main task performance did **not** negatively affect perceived safety or other factors, suggesting users tolerate transient minor errors if functionality is maintained.

#### Objective Measures for Evaluating and Predicting Perceived Safety

- The study employed physiological signals (**EDA**, **heart rate variability**) collected via **wristbands** and **facial affect analysis** from video recordings **alongside questionnaires** to evaluate perceived safety. #measures/objective_measures 
- **Physiological data provided higher accuracy in predicting perceived safety** (up to 79% accuracy) compared to facial affect data, indicating stronger promise for objective monitoring of safety perception. #perception/safety

- **Positive emotional valence** correlates with higher perceived safety, though discrete emotions like **anger** and **fear** did not show correlation in this study. #cognitive/fear #cognitive/anger
