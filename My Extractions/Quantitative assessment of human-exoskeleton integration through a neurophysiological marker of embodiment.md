# Quantitative assessment of human-exoskeleton integration through a neurophysiological marker of embodiment

**The study shows that human–exoskeleton integration can be objectively measured using a neurophysiological marker of embodiment, demonstrating that exoskeleton use alters brain activity patterns linked to [[Body Ownership]] and motor control. ** #measures/objective_measures/embodiment  ^integration-measured-by-embodiment

The more expected movement and actual movement match (i.e., **spatiotemporal** **congruency**), the more the subject experiences a ==**sense of agency**== over that movement, a ==**sense of self-location**== with the body part or tool used, and a ==**sense of ownership**== over the action. In contrast, a consistent mismatch will lead to a decreased tool ==**embodiment**==.

## Key Findings

- **Embodiment Marker Identified**
    
    - Researchers developed an **EEG-based neurophysiological marker** that quantifies how strongly users perceive the exoskeleton as part of their body.
    - This marker reflects **sensorimotor integration** and changes in brain activity during exoskeleton use.
- **Integration with the Human Body**
    
    - Exoskeleton use produced **altered brain activity patterns** consistent with enhanced **body ownership** and **motor agency**.
    - Users showed evidence of treating the exoskeleton as an extension of themselves.
- **Objective Measurement**
    
    - Unlike subjective questionnaires, this marker provides **quantifiable, reproducible evidence** of embodiment.
    - It can track **adaptation over time**, showing how quickly and effectively users integrate the exoskeleton into their body schema.
- **Rehabilitation Applications**
    
    - Embodiment is essential for effective rehabilitation: patients must feel the device is part of their body to regain motor function.
    - This marker can help clinicians **personalize therapy**, monitor progress, and optimize exoskeleton design for better acceptance.
- **Assistive Robotics Implications**
    
    - Embodiment ensures **natural and intuitive use** of assistive devices.
    - Measuring embodiment could become a **standard evaluation tool** for human–robot integration, extending to prosthetics, VR, and other interfaces.

---

## Practical Implications

- **Clinical Use**: Provides a way to objectively measure whether patients are truly integrating exoskeletons into their motor system.
- **Device Design**: Insights can guide engineers to create exoskeletons that maximize embodiment and minimize rejection.
- **Training Protocols**: Helps tailor rehabilitation programs to individual users, improving recovery outcomes.
- **Broader Robotics**: Could extend to prosthetics, brain–computer interfaces, and immersive technologies where embodiment is critical.

---

## Conclusion

This research is among the first to demonstrate that **embodiment can be measured neurophysiologically in exoskeleton use**. By linking brain activity to body ownership, it provides a **scientific foundation for evaluating and improving human–robot integration**, paving the way for more effective rehabilitation and assistive technologies.

***exoskeleton use → brain activity changes → embodiment → rehabilitation outcomes***
