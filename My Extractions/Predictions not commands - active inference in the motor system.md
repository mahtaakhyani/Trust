### Summary of Notes on Proprioception

- **Proprioceptive Prediction and Spinal Reflex Arc**  
  Motor cortical descending signals convey predictions of proprioceptive input including muscle spindle, Golgi tendon organ, and joint receptor states. These proprioceptive predictions are compared with actual sensory inputs in the spinal cord to generate proprioceptive prediction errors. These errors are uniquely resolved by action through activation of alpha motor neurons in the spinal reflex arc, converting sensory prediction errors into movement (Spinal inverse mapping section). #proprioception #prediction_error ^tr-n6hg01kds

- **Role of Proprioceptive Feedback**  
  Accurate proprioceptive feedback is crucial for proper motor control. Loss of proprioception disrupts spinal inverse mapping, causing impairments in movement coordination, as shown in deafferented patients who have difficulty coordinating joint movements without visual feedback (Empirical studies on deafferented patients). #proprioception/proprioceptive_feedback ^tr-fa1uzlfeq

- **Proprioceptive Predictions as Backward (Modulatory) Signals**  
  Descending projections from motor cortex encode proprioceptive predictions modeled as backward or modulatory signals in the predictive coding hierarchy, in contrast to driving sensory input ascending projections. These predictions await confirmation or correction by sensory feedback but primarily aim to be fulfilled by the reflex arc rather than corrected at the cortical level (Backward signaling section). #proprioception/proprioceptive_predictions ^tr-no1bpz73s

- **Implications of Proprioceptive Precision and Gain Modulation**  
  Corticomotoneuronal neurons encode both first-order proprioceptive predictions and second-order predictions of their precision (gain), which modulate alpha and gamma motor neuron activity. This gain control mechanism allows for context-sensitive regulation of reflex responses and sensorimotor integration, influencing muscle tone and movement execution (CM neurons and precision section). #proprioception/bio/Corticomotoneuronal_neurons #proprioception/bio/gamma_motor_neuron ^tr-zugl5cfzy

- **Effects of Proprioceptive Loss or Dysfunction**  
  Proprioceptive deafferentation leads to flaccid paralysis because the spinal cord lacks the sensory feedback necessary for generating proprioceptive prediction errors that drive alpha motor neurons. Conversely, lesions interfering with supraspinal inhibition can cause spasticity due to maladaptive precision signaling and increased stretch reflex gain (Clinical insights on proprioception). 

- **Compensation by Cortical Forward Models**  
  In cases where proprioceptive feedback is lost, higher cortical forward models can partially compensate using visual feedback to maintain movement, highlighting the interplay between sensory modalities in producing smooth motor control (Compensation mechanisms in deafferented patients).

- **Proprioception in Context of Motor Control Theories**  
  Under active inference, proprioceptive predictions replace explicit motor commands and are fulfilled via spinal reflexes, simplifying the inverse problem of motor control and providing a unifying explanation for proprioceptive function in movement generation (Motor control theoretical implications).

> [!note] In Simpler Words:
> Under the framework of **active inference** (*a theory in neuroscience where the brain continuously generates predictions about incoming sensory signals and acts to minimize the difference between predicted and actual signals*), movement is controlled in a novel way. Instead of sending detailed **motor commands** (*explicit instructions from the brain to muscles telling them how strongly to contract*), the brain generates **proprioceptive predictions** (*expectations about the sensory feedback from the body’s position and movement*).
   These predictions are then carried out by **spinal reflexes** (automatic circuits in the spinal cord that adjust muscle activity without conscious effort). In this way, the body fulfills the brain’s predictions locally, rather than requiring the brain to solve the complex **inverse problem of motor control** (*the mathematical challenge of determining which exact muscle activations will produce a desired movement*).
   This mechanism provides a unified explanation for the role of **proprioception** in generating movement: **proprioception is not just passive sensing, but an active part of how movements are initiated and controlled**.
