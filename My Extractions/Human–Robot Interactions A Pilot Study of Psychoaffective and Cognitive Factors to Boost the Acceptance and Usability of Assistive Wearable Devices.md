
# Human–Robot Interactions: A Pilot Study of Psychoaffective and Cognitive Factors to Boost the Acceptance and Usability of Assistive Wearable Devices 
[link](https://doi.org/10.3390/mti9010005)

**The study found that anxiety and cognitive reserve significantly shape how people perceive workload and usability when using assistive exoskeletons: state anxiety after use predicted overall workload, trait anxiety predicted effort, and higher cognitive reserve was linked to better usability perceptions. #robot/exoskeleton #perception/usability/factors/cognitive_reserve #cognitive/cognitive_reserve #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety 
Familiarization reduced anxiety, and frustration negatively correlated with perceived stimulation, highlighting the importance of tailoring robotic rehabilitation to psychoaffective and cognitive profiles.**

## Key Findings

- **Anxiety Effects**
    
    - **State anxiety (after exoskeleton use)** predicted higher perceived workload (**NASA-TLX total score**, Adjusted R² = 0.45, p = 0.02). #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety #measures/objective_measures/NASA-TLX #perception/workload/factors/anxiety
    - **Trait anxiety** predicted perceived effort (Adjusted R² = 0.43, p = 0.02). #trust_factors/human_factors/traits/anxiety #perception/effort/trait_anxiety
    - **Pre-trial anxiety** was significantly higher than post-trial anxiety, showing that **familiarization reduces anxiety**. #trust_factors/human_factors/familiarity #cognitive/anxiety/state_anxiety/factors/familiarization ^c1d74f
    - Anxiety did **not** predict usability scores (AttrakDiff). #measures/objective_measures/AttrakDiff ^tr-7ak7uivu1
    
- **Cognitive Reserve**
    
    - Cognitive reserve (measured by CRIq) did **not** predict workload. #cognitive/cognitive_reserve 
    - It did predict **pragmatic quality (PQ)** of usability (Adjusted R² = 0.45, p = 0.02).
    - Participants with higher cognitive reserve perceived the device as **less complex and more motivating**. #perception/usability/factors/cognitive_reserve ^tr-20bv9wpox
    
- **Motor Performance**
    - Motor performance (time donning the exoskeleton, number of steps) did not significantly predict workload or usability. #trust_factors/robot_factors/performance #motor_control/motor_performance
    - **Tendencies**: longer donning time → higher physical demand; more steps → higher perceived attractiveness. #perception/attractiveness/factors/num_of_steps ^tr-d1za7im2f
    
- **Workload vs. Usability**
    
    - Significant **negative correlation** between **frustration (NASA-TLX)** and **hedonic quality—stimulation (AttrakDiff)** (r = –0.67, p = 0.03).  
        → People who found the device stimulating experienced less frustration. #perception/frustration/factors/stimulating_device ^tr-4bd97906g

## Practical Implications

- **Familiarization sessions** are crucial to reduce anxiety before rehabilitation.
- **Trait anxiety assessment** can help anticipate effort perception and tailor support.
- **Cognitive reserve evaluation** may guide user selection, as higher reserve predicts better usability perception.
- **Device design and training protocols** should minimize frustration and emphasize stimulation to improve acceptance.

---

## Conclusion

This **pilot study** provides preliminary evidence that **psychoaffective (anxiety) and cognitive (reserve) factors** strongly influence how users experience workload and usability in robotic rehabilitation. A **human-centric approach**—considering psychological and cognitive profiles—is essential for effective integration of exoskeletons into rehabilitation programs.
